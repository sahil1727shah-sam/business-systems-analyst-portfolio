* **Agile vs Scrum vs Kanban — the relationship**



1. Agile = the mindset/values



2\. Scrum = the most common framework implementing Agile — fixed-length sprints, defined roles, defined ceremonies.

* Scrum fits planned feature development 



3\. Kanban = a different Agile framework — continuous flow, no fixed sprints, just a visual board with WIP limits (limiting how many items can be "in progress" at once to avoid bottlenecks

* Kanban fits support/maintenance work where tickets arrive unpredictably (bug fixes, ad hoc requests) — there's no "sprint" to plan around.





* **Scrum artifacts (the 3 things Scrum manages)**



(Artifact	What it is	BA's role)



1. Product Backlog: Everything that could ever be built, prioritized
* BA writes/refines items with the PO



2\. Sprint Backlog: The subset the team commits to for this sprint	

* BA clarifies scope before commitment



3\. Increment: The actual working output at sprint's end	

* BA validates it matches the requirement



* **Scrum Process**



Sprint Planning > Product Backlog > Sprint Backlog > Daily SCRUM (Sprint) > Sprint Review (Shippable Product) > Sprint Retrospective





* **Scrum ceremonies — what actually happens in each**



1. Sprint Planning — team picks backlog items for the sprint, estimates effort (often "story points," a relative sizing scale, not hours)

2\. Daily Stand-up — 15 min, 3 questions: what I did, what I'm doing, any blockers. BA reports on requirement clarifications given/needed

3\. Sprint Review — demo the built increment to stakeholders; BA checks it actually solves the original business problem, not just "does it technically work"

4\. Sprint Retrospective — team-only, reflects on process ("what slowed us down," "what should we keep doing")



* **Roles — the interview-critical distinction**
1. Scrum Master — protects the process, removes blockers, doesn't decide what gets built

2\. Product Owner — owns what gets built and in what order (business priority decisions)

3\. Business Analyst — does the deep elicitation and documentation that feeds the PO's 4. decisions; writes/refines stories and acceptance criteria



NOTE: "The PO decides what to build and why. The BA figures out exactly what that means in detail — requirements, edge cases, acceptance criteria — so the dev team isn't guessing."



* **User Stories**
1. A very high level definition of  what the customer wants the system to do. 

2\. Each story is captured as a separate item on the Product Backlog

3\. User stories are NOT dependent on other stories

**Story Template:**

* “As a <User> I want <function> So that <desired result>
* Story Example:
* As a user, I want to print a recipe so that I can cook it.



* **Story Points**
1. A simple way to initially estimate level of effort expected to develop

2\. Story points are a relative measure of feature difficulty

3\. Usually scored on a scale of 1-10.  1=very easy through 10=very difficult

**Example:**

“Send to a Friend” Story Points = 2

“Shopping Cart” Story Points = 9 



What are stories, epics, and initiatives?

• **Stories**, also called “user stories,” are short requirements or requests written from the perspective of an end user.

• **Epics** are large bodies of work that can be broken down into a number of smaller tasks (called stories).

• Initiatives are collections of epics that drive toward a common goal.
